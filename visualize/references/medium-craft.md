Which canvas you are on changes what to build, not only how to build it. A board built for one medium and ported to the other lands noticeably worse, so read the section for the destination in front of you.

## A precise design canvas

Favours dense typographic cards, tight alignment, deliberate spacing and fixed frames. This is the medium for something that will be read closely, exported, or presented, where the reader consumes the board rather than touching it.

Here a diagram is an arrangement: shapes placed exactly, edges drawn as lines, the whole composition balanced. Text can be long, because a card can hold a paragraph and still look intentional. Reuse whatever text styles and colour variables the file already defines rather than inventing new ones.

## A collaborative whiteboard

Favours notes, simple shapes and connectors. This is the medium for something a group will annotate, vote on and rearrange, where the board is the meeting rather than a record of one.

Build from pieces people can move. A decision tree here is real connected shapes, so that someone can trace an edge with a finger and add a branch you did not think of. Content lives in movable notes rather than fixed frames, and a connector is a real connector rather than a drawn line, so it survives the shape being dragged.

## Choosing when the link does not say

Default to the whiteboard. A team gathering to discuss a plan will want to add to it.
